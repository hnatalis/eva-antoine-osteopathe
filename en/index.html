An animal in discomfort or in pain does not tell you: it adapts.

Pain is one of the main reasons people consult an osteopath. Yet although animals are just as exposed to minor injuries, falls, awkward movements and stress, they rarely show their pain or their distress in any obvious way.

That is why you will find here a few signs, species by species, that sometimes go unnoticed.

The signs, species by species

Getting the body moving again, with one tool only: our hands.

Osteopathy is a manual therapy that aims to restore the body’s balance by working, through a range of techniques, on different anatomical structures: joints, muscles, fascia and viscera. This whole-body, natural, preventive and curative approach complements conventional veterinary care by helping your animal regain a better quality of life.

Five years of study, and one conviction: osteopathy is practised alongside the vet.

Animals and their wellbeing have always been my passion. I discovered animal osteopathy in 2015, during a placement at the veterinary clinic of the Cadre Noir in Saumur. It was a revelation, and it led me to the C-NESOA in Châtel-Guyon for five years of training.

After graduating in 2024, I practised for three months near Rome, then for a year at the Roland-Garros veterinary clinic in Boulogne-Billancourt, in osteopathy and small-animal rehabilitation.
