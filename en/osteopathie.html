Getting the body moving again, with the hands.

Osteopathy is a manual therapy that aims to restore the body’s balance by working, through a range of techniques, on different anatomical structures: joints, muscles, fascia and viscera.

Understanding the animal as a whole.

A session always begins with observation: posture, weight-bearing, the way the animal moves and the way it accepts being touched.

A whole-body approach.

Every animal is unique, and its balance rests on many elements that interact constantly: its physical condition, its habits, its activity, the demands placed on it day to day, its environment, its history, its behaviour, and any underlying conditions.

When one area works less well, or differently, the body can put in place adaptations and compensations. These can in turn alter posture, movement, or the way the animal uses its body. A problem in the bones and joints, the muscles, the nervous system or the organs can have repercussions throughout the whole body. In the same way, pain, stress or certain everyday or work-related demands can influence its behaviour and the way it moves.

The aim of osteopathy is therefore not to treat only “the spot that hurts”, but to understand the animal as a whole, and the adaptations its body has put in place, so as to offer care suited to its situation and its needs.

A complementary therapy.

Animal osteopathy is part of care that complements veterinary medicine. It replaces neither the diagnosis, nor the treatment, nor the medical follow-up carried out by the vet, but it can accompany them. Or, in some cases, be contraindicated.

Talking with the other professionals involved with the animal therefore helps to support consistent, whole-body care.

Caring for your animal means acting for its wellbeing today, and also heading off tomorrow’s imbalances.

Every animal has its own anatomy and physiology, and must constantly adapt to varied demands throughout its life.

Imbalances and dysfunctions can therefore set in gradually, often silently, without immediately causing visible signs. It is only when the body’s capacity to adapt is disturbed or exceeded that symptoms may appear.

Symptoms are therefore often only the visible part of a deeper imbalance that has already taken hold. This is why a preventive approach can help preserve your animal’s balance, comfort and mobility.
